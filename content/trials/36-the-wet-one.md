**THE LINE**

Genuine rain, on purpose, for a session you'd normally reschedule. Not because miserable is
good for you — because the postponing habit needs testing occasionally.

**THE WORK**

1. Pick a session you've already done here — a walk, the Stack outdoors, anything suited to being outside.
2. Do it in real rain — not a light drizzle you'd have gone out in anyway, actual rain you'd normally use as a reason to stay in.
3. Dress for it properly: waterproof layers, footwear that can get wet without becoming dangerous.
4. Same effort and duration as you'd use for that session any other day. The weather is the variable, not the difficulty.
5. Dry off and get warm properly as soon as you're done — that's part of the trial, not an afterthought.

**THE RUBRIC**

*RISK* — Getting cold rather than just wet, especially if you stop moving outdoors in wet
clothes. Reduced visibility and grip underfoot, particularly on leaves, painted surfaces or
wet stone.

*GROUND* — A route you know well enough to manage in reduced visibility, without unlit roads
or ground that gets genuinely treacherous when wet. **Never** in thunderstorms, however light
the rain — that's postpone, not scale. Never in flooding of any kind.

*STOP* — The usual four constants, plus: stop and get warm and dry immediately if you start
shivering or can't warm up, and turn back if thunder starts.

*SCALE* — A shorter session than usual, same rain · rain seen through an open window during
an indoor session, if genuine outdoor rain isn't safely available to you where you are.

*CLEARED WHEN* — The session is done, in real rain, dried off and warmed up afterward.

**NOTE**

The getting-dressed part turns out to be the actual obstacle, most weeks.
