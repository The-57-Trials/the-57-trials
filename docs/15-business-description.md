# 15. What the business does

The canonical description. **Use these consistently** — Stripe checks its version against the
website, and an insurer can void a policy over a description that understates the risk. Where
they differ below, the difference is deliberate.

---

## The one-line

> **The 57 Trials is a paid online membership that delivers fifty-seven written physical
> challenges, taken strictly in order over five to nine months, with milestone merchandise
> posted along the way.**

---

## For Stripe (account activation)

Must match the website. Stripe reviewers compare the two.

> The 57 Trials is a paid online membership operating at the57trials.com. Members pay a
> one-time £19.57 Entry Pass and an optional £9.57/month Circuit Pass. In return they receive
> access to a sequence of fifty-seven written challenge briefings, released strictly in order,
> which members complete in their own time and location. Physical merchandise — a printed
> bib, patches and a finisher item — is posted to members at four progress milestones and is
> included in the price rather than sold separately. All content is delivered digitally
> through the website. The business is UK-based and sells to UK consumers.

**Say "online membership" and "written briefings". Do not say "fitness coaching", "personal
training", "training programme" or "transformation".** Those put you in a
higher-scrutiny category, imply supervision you do not provide, and would contradict your own
liability position.

**Also avoid anything Stripe treats as a restricted practice:** outcome guarantees, "get fit
fast", testimonial-led claims, or a free trial that auto-converts. Keep claims about
*difficulty*, never about *results*.

---

## For insurers and brokers

Deliberately blunt. **Non-disclosure voids cover**, and an insurer who thinks you supervise
people has not underwritten your actual risk.

> I sell a paid online membership. Members receive written physical-challenge briefings and
> complete them **unsupervised, alone, in their own time and location**. There is **no
> supervision, no assessment and no verification** of what they do. Members **self-declare**
> that they are fit to take part; I do not collect or hold medical information. I have no
> in-person contact with members at any point. I am a UK sole trader selling to UK consumers.

Then ask, and get the answers **in writing**:

1. Does this policy cover claims arising from injury sustained by a member performing an activity described in my written content, with no supervision from me?
2. Are there exclusions for unsupervised or remote participation?
3. *(If in-person events are ever likely)* Can the policy be extended to cover organised in-person events in the UK, and what would that add?

---

## For the public

Brand voice. Register, not marketing.

> **Fifty-seven trials, in order, on your word.**
>
> The 57 Trials is a fixed sequence of fifty-seven challenges, taken strictly in order over
> five to nine months. The order is enforced and the intervals between trials are compulsory.
> Nothing you do is ever checked — the record stands on your word.
>
> You are issued a bib number when you join. It is yours permanently and is never reissued.

Three things this deliberately does **not** claim: that it will make you fit, that it is easy,
or that anyone is watching.

---

## For an accountant, bank or Companies House

Plain and factual.

> Online subscription membership service. Sells digital written content by subscription to UK
> consumers, with associated branded merchandise included in the membership price. Sole
> trader, not VAT registered. Payments processed by Stripe.

**SIC code is an accountant question, not one to guess at.** Candidates worth putting to them
are the publishing and web-portal classifications rather than the sports-instruction ones —
sports instruction implies teaching and supervision, which is precisely the framing to avoid.
Ask them to confirm.

---

## What the business is *not*

Useful for keeping every description honest, and for knowing when a description has drifted.

- **Not coaching or personal training.** No individual guidance, no plans adjusted to a person, no supervision. The moment you advise an individual on their own circumstances, the scope framing collapses and the duty of care widens.
- **Not a fitness product.** Roughly half the trials are not physical at all — reflection, consistency, skill and social. Overselling the fitness angle attracts the wrong buyer and raises the refund rate.
- **Not verified.** Completion is self-reported and always will be. This is the product, not a gap in it.
- **Not a community platform.** No member-to-member content at launch, deliberately — that would bring Online Safety Act duties.
- **Not a charity or fundraiser** in its own right. If Charity Trials go ahead, 100% reaches the charity through a regulated platform the business never touches money in.

---

## Keep these in step

If the offer changes, all four descriptions change together. The most common way this goes
wrong is the public copy drifting into outcome claims while the Stripe and insurance
descriptions stay accurate — which is the worst combination, because the marketing is what a
regulator or claimant reads first.

---

## When the Reckoning ships (Phase 2)

At launch, completion is **purely self-attested** and every description above is accurate as
written.

When member witnessing goes live, three of them need revisiting — but **less than you might
expect**, because witness is not verification:

> A witness **attests they saw it**. A reviewer **judges whether it was good enough**.

The business still verifies nothing and assesses nothing. Members attest to each other; there
is no approval and no rejection. So the insurer wording — *no supervision, no assessment and
no verification* — **remains true**, which is the one that matters most.

What does change:

| Description | Change needed |
|---|---|
| Public | May describe witnessing. Must not imply completions are *checked*. |
| Stripe | Note that the service includes member-submitted content. |
| Insurer | **Notify them.** The risk barely moves, but non-disclosure is how policies get voided. |
| "What it is not" | "Not a community platform" stops being true. |

### The line not to cross

**Never let the Reckoning drift from attestation into approval.** The moment members can
*reject* a completion, or the moment the business arbitrates a disputed one, it has taken on a
checking role — and the brand promise and the liability posture both change with it.

*"I have read this and I take it as given"* is load-bearing wording. It is not
*"I approve this."*
