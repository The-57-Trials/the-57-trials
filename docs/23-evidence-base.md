## 6. Compulsion is the one thing in this product that fights the evidence

Self-determination theory is unambiguous: intrinsic motivation is undermined by **control,
deadlines, surveillance and excessive reward**, and supported by **autonomy and competence**.
Chosen participation and mandated participation produce different outcomes even when the
activity is identical.

**Almost every design decision already made is autonomy-supportive**, which is unusual:

| Design | SDT reading |
|---|---|
| No deadline — a trial stays open forever | Removes the single most-cited underminer |
| SCALE always clears | Choice inside the task; competence support |
| Nobody checks, nothing is rejected | No surveillance |
| No leaderboard (D8) | No controlling comparison |
| Merch is earned, never dangled | Avoids the over-justification effect |

**THE ACCOUNT is the exception, and it is compulsory.** That is a real cost, taken knowingly
for the book. The mitigations are not optional:

- **No word count, no quality signal, no progress bar on the fields.** A 40-character floor and nothing else. The moment the UI implies a standard, the account becomes a test.
- **Never evaluated, never surfaced back as a score,** and the page must say so.
- **The content is entirely theirs.** Fixed prompts, unconstrained answers.
- **No time limit on writing it.** It waits.

> Keep the requirement. Remove every trace of judgement around it.

## 7. The quiet trials are the hardest thing in the product, not the easy ones

Wilson et al. ran eleven studies and found people disliked 6–15 minutes alone with their own
thoughts so much that, having previously said they would **pay money to avoid an electric
shock**, **67% of men and 25% of women shocked themselves rather than sit there.** One
participant did it 190 times. It held across ages 18–77.

**Consequence.** FIRST GROUND's no-headphones rule, SILENT HOUR, THE BOREDOM SIT and SOLITUDE
are not filler between the physical trials. For most people they are **harder than the
walking**, and the briefings must say so plainly rather than presenting them as rest. It also
means they belong on the difficulty curve as real steps, and that the grading cohort will
probably rank them higher than we expect.

**Safety.** For some people unstructured rumination is genuinely harmful. The MARK signpost
footer (19.1) applies to every quiet trial, not only the writing ones.

## 8. There is a real dose for outdoors, and it is 120 minutes a week

White et al., **n = 19,806**: people getting **at least 120 minutes a week** in nature were
significantly more likely to report good health and high wellbeing. **Below 120 minutes there
was no benefit at all** — and it made no difference whether it came in one visit or several.

**Consequence.** This is the most quotable number available to the product, and the trials
should be built to clear it rather than accidentally land near it. FIRST GROUND is 30
minutes, THE HOUR is 60, THE THIRD HOUR is 180. **From chapter III the outdoor trials should
put a member over 120 minutes in the weeks they fall.**

It is also a **legitimate results claim** — a threshold from a 19,806-person study, not a
promise about their body.

## 9. "Stopping costs you nothing" is not softness — it outperforms

Breines & Chen, four experiments: people put in a self-compassionate frame after a failure
**studied longer for a re-test, believed weaknesses were more changeable, and reported more
motivation to change** than those in control or self-esteem conditions. Self-criticism
predicts procrastination, avoidance and self-handicapping.

**Consequence.** The most distinctive line in the briefings — *stopping costs you nothing,
the trial stays open, scaled counts* — is the best-evidenced motivational choice in the
product. Keep it in every rubric.

**One correction it forces.** Trial 10 currently reads *"miss one and it restarts."* Restart
is fine as arithmetic — ten consecutive days means ten consecutive days — but it must never
be framed as a penalty. Write it as a fact, never as a consequence, and never pair it with
language about failing.

## 10. A fifteen-minute instruction turns a walk into something else

Sturm et al.: older adults taking a **15-minute weekly "awe walk" for eight weeks** reported
increased prosocial positive emotions — compassion, gratitude — and less daily distress,
against a control group taking identical walks. The mechanism is **"small self"**: a
proportion between yourself and everything else. Their selfies drifted from their own faces
to their surroundings, and their smiles measurably widened.

**Consequence, and it is free.** The difference between the two groups was **one instruction**
— attend to what is around you and its scale. Adding that line to FIRST GROUND, THE HOUR, THE
RETURN and THE THIRD HOUR costs one sentence and is the only evidence-backed "spirit"
mechanism found so far that requires no belief in anything.

It also answers what a member does during THE HOUR with nothing in their ears.
