**THE LINE**

A mile, walked as slowly as you can manage without stopping. Everything else here has asked
for more; this one asks for less, on purpose.

**THE WORK**

1. Walk a mile, continuously, at the slowest pace you can sustain without actually stopping.
2. No target time — the instruction is "as slow as possible while still moving," not a specific number of minutes.
3. Notice how strange deliberately slow feels compared to every other trial here that's asked you to sustain effort.
4. If you catch yourself speeding up out of habit, that's worth noticing, not fighting. Just slow back down.
5. Finish the mile, however long it took.

**THE RUBRIC**

*RISK* — Almost none, physically. If anything, the risk is boredom making you quit early
rather than any physical strain.

*GROUND* — A flat, safe route you know, ideally one where walking unusually slowly won't put
you in anyone's way.

*STOP* — The usual four constants, though at this pace they're unlikely to arise from the
walking itself: chest pain or tightness · breathlessness that doesn't settle within a minute
of stopping · dizziness that doesn't clear when you sit down · any sharp or new pain.

*SCALE* — Half a mile instead of a full one, at the same deliberately slow pace.

*CLEARED WHEN* — The mile, or half-mile scale, is finished at a pace slower than you'd
normally choose.

**NOTE**

This is the only trial in the book where going faster would mean doing it wrong.
