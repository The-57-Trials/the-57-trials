# 20. Physical trials — what is actually available

> 1 Sep 2026. Written after doc 19 showed only six of ~forty bank items surviving, and Rob
> asked what it would take to have real physical challenges.

---

## 20.1 The exclusions forbid intensity, not difficulty

Read them again and sort them. Every one of the global exclusions in 4.1 is either:

- **Intensity** — maximal or to-failure work, one-rep maxes, timed distance targets, continuing through pain.
- **Exposure** — cold, heat, water, heights, breath-holding, fasting, dehydration, sleep restriction.
- **Isolation** — unspotted barbell work, anywhere help could not reach.

**Not one of them limits volume, duration or load at a controlled effort.** That axis is
wide open, and it is where hard actually lives for the people this product is for.

The bank scored badly because commercial "challenge" content is built almost entirely on
intensity and exposure — that is the genre. It is not evidence that the exclusions are too
tight. It is evidence that the bank was written for a different product.

> **Hard by quantity is allowed. Hard by intensity is not.**
> A hundred reps across a day at ten a time is brutal and breaks nothing. Five reps to
> failure is trivial by comparison and is excluded.

---

## 20.2 Twelve demanding MOVE trials that break no exclusion

These are compliant **as written**, today, with no change to 4.1 and no insurance
conversation. Every one has a scale that clears.

| Working title | The work | Why it is hard |
|---|---|---|
| **THE HOUR** | Sixty unbroken minutes moving. Any modality. Talkable pace throughout. | Duration. Twice FIRST GROUND, and the pace rule makes speeding it up illegal. |
| **THE RETURN** | Walk out for forty-five minutes. Then walk back. | You cannot quit at the halfway point. The trial is the second half, and it is the first real commitment device in the run. |
| **THE HUNDRED** | One hundred reps of one movement across a day. Never more than ten in a set. | Volume without a single hard set. The set cap is what keeps it legal *and* what makes it take all day. |
| **EVERY HOUR** | Every waking hour, twenty reps. Twelve hours or more. | It owns your entire day. Nothing is strenuous; everything is interrupted. |
| **THE ASCENT** | A fixed elevation — 300m — of stairs or hill. No time limit, handrail in reach. | Quantity, not pace. Genuinely demanding and completely uncompetitive. |
| **THE LOADED HOUR** | Carry 15% of bodyweight for an hour. Set it down as often as you like. | THE CARRY grown up. The permission to set it down is what keeps it safe and does not make it easy. |
| **THE DOUBLE** | Two separate sessions in one day. Morning and evening, four hours apart. | The second one is the trial. Nobody wants to change twice. |
| **BEFORE LIGHT** | A full session completed before sunrise. | In a British winter this is a 6am start in the dark. No intensity required at all. |
| **THE WET ONE** | Go out on purpose in weather you would normally postpone for. | Deliberately doing the thing you would avoid. AMBER already carries weather clauses. |
| **THE SLOW MILE** | One mile as *slowly* as you can while still walking continuously. | Inverts the entire genre. Impossible to race, unexpectedly horrible, and completely safe. |
| **THE THIRD HOUR** | Three hours on your feet outdoors. Food and water carried. Sit whenever you want. | The ASCENSION idiom, arriving early enough to be a real step up. |
| **THE REPEAT, LOADED** | Trial 06 again, with a loaded bag. Same reps, same pace. | Progression by load rather than by effort. The honest way to get stronger in this system. |

**THE SLOW MILE and THE RETURN are the two most on-brand physical trials in this document.**
Both are hard in ways no other product's challenge list can be, precisely *because* of the
constraints. Neither could exist in a product with a leaderboard.

## 20.3 The unlock you have not used: supervision

Every exclusion was written assuming **solo and unsupervised**, because that is the default.
A trial can require otherwise, and doing so moves the risk into a setting that is
professionally managed, insured and staffed.

This reopens whole categories with the risk owned by someone qualified:

| Trial | Setting | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **THE POOL** | Any lifeguarded public pool. Thirty minutes in the water, any stroke, rest on the side whenever. | The bank's Swim Trial was excluded for submersion with nobody present. A lifeguard is *literally* the person whose job that is. |
| **THE WALL** | A commercial climbing centre. One induction session. | Excluded as heights; permitted with mats, staff, a harness and an instructor. Also expensive — make it optional. |
| **THE CLASS** | Any coached session in something you have never done. | A qualified coach is present and screening you. This is the safest physical trial in the whole product. |
| **THE FIVE** | A parkrun. Walk it, jog it, finish last, nobody minds. | Free, in almost every UK town, marshalled, and **already the brand's stated reference point** (doc 12). Walking it is explicitly normal there, so the scale is built into the event rather than into our copy. |

**THE FIVE is the strongest addition available to the product.** It is free, it is supervised
by definition, it is the only trial that puts a member physically among other people doing
the same thing, and it borrows the credibility of the organisation the brand already
positions itself alongside.

> **Do not require any trial that costs money.** A pool is a few pounds and a climbing
> induction is £25 or more. THE FIVE is free. Where a paid setting is used, the scale must
> always be a free alternative — a member's progress can never depend on their ability to pay
> for a venue.

## 20.4 What relaxing an exclusion would actually cost

Two of the exclusions are broader than their risk. Narrowing them is defensible — but not
before the insurance is in place, because these are the exact lines an insurer will ask about.

**"Timed distance targets."** The real risk is *racing* — a member pushing because of a
number. That risk is already dead: D8 removed the leaderboard, D2 made the pace
uncompetitive, and Trial 15 already sets a duration. The exclusion could narrow to **"no time
to beat, and no time ever compared between members"**, which permits *"keep moving for sixty
minutes"* and still forbids *"run a mile as fast as you can."* **Low risk, recommend after
insurance.**

**"Heights."** Written for unsupervised height work, which should stay excluded forever. It
also blocks a commercial climbing wall with mats, staff and a harness, which is a completely
different risk class. Could narrow to **"no unsupervised height work."** **Medium risk,
recommend only with the insurer's sign-off in writing.**

**Cold exposure — hold this one.** Cold showers are low-risk for healthy adults and cold
water immersion is not, but the distinction is far too easy for a member to blur on their own
in a briefing they half-read. The gap between what you wrote and what someone does is the
whole problem, and it is not worth reopening for a cold shower. **Keep excluded.**

**Everything else stays.** Breath-holding, open water, fasting, dehydration, sleep
restriction, maximal work and to-failure work should never come back, at any insurance
price. They are the exclusions that stop this product hurting someone.

## 20.5 What to do

1. **Write the twelve in 20.2 first.** They need no permission from anybody, they are available today, and they get MOVE from six survivors to eighteen.
2. **Add THE FIVE.** Free, supervised, on-brand, and the best single physical trial available to this product.
3. **Take 20.4 to the insurer** as two specific questions rather than an open one. "May we permit a commercial climbing induction?" gets an answer. "What physical activity may we permit?" gets a refusal.
4. **Leave the remaining exclusions alone.**
