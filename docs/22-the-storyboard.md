## 22.1 Five findings, and what each one changes

### 1. The run is long enough for habits to actually form — most challenges are not
Lally et al. (2010) tracked 96 people forming a daily habit: **mean 66 days to automaticity,
range 18–254.** The famous "21 days" is a myth with no evidence behind it.

**Consequence.** Almost every commercial challenge — 30-day, 75-day — ends *before* the
average habit forms. At **195 days** (D15) this product runs past the mean and into the long
tail. That is not a marketing claim, it is arithmetic, and it is the strongest defensible
thing about the format. **Say it: "Most challenges end before the habit forms. This one
doesn't."**

### 2. Compulsory writing has real evidence behind it — if it is framed correctly
Frattaroli's (2006) meta-analysis found expressive writing associated with fewer physician
visits, behaviour change and long-term mood improvement; **sessions over 15 minutes were more
potent than shorter ones.** Later meta-analyses are more equivocal, and a 2023 study found
outcomes improve materially with **emotion-acceptance framing and writer engagement.**

**Consequence for THE ACCOUNT (doc 21).** The evidence does not support "write anything."
It supports writing that is long enough, engaged, and framed as *accepting* what happened
rather than reporting it. **WHERE IT GOT HARD is the field that does the work** — keep it,
and never let the UI imply a minimum-viable answer. Do not claim health benefits to members.

### 3. The pledge is an implementation intention, and it is currently written wrong
Gollwitzer & Sheeran's meta-analysis of 94 studies: forming if-then plans produced
**d = 0.65 on goal attainment and d = 0.77 on preventing derailment** — the single
best-evidenced self-regulation technique there is. Effects are larger when the plan is in
**contingent if-then format**, when motivation is high, and when the plan is **rehearsed at
least once.**

**Consequence.** Trial 03 currently asks for "a sentence you will say." That leaves the
strongest part on the table. It must become an explicit **if-then**: *"If [the specific
moment I will want to stop], then I will [the specific thing I will do]."* Rehearsal aloud is
already in the trial and is evidence-backed — keep it and say why.

> **This is a rewrite of a trial already written and loaded. It is worth it: d = 0.77 against
> derailment is the whole retention problem, addressed by changing one sentence's grammar.**

### 4. The book is a narrative-identity intervention — but do not force the arc
McAdams' redemption-sequence work: life stories told as bad-to-good correlate with
psychological wellbeing, contamination sequences with the opposite. **Critical caveat from
the literature: the redemption arc is culturally American**, interwoven with US religious and
recovery narratives, and non-American samples show weaker and qualitatively different effects.

**Consequence.** The book is well-founded — 57 accounts *are* a structured life narrative.
But this is a **UK product**, and a triumphant redemption template would ring false here. The
austere register is not just brand preference, it is the culturally correct instrument. **The
book must let a member's story be what it was**, including flat or unresolved. Never
auto-generate uplift.

### 5. Give a real head start, because impatience is fatal early
Nunes & Drèze (2006): loyalty cards needing eight purchases completed at **19%**, versus
**34%** for a ten-stamp card pre-stamped twice. Identical work, nearly double completion.

**Consequence, and it answers Rob's concern directly.** Do not fake progress — this product
cannot. Give a **real** one: **Trial 01 is completed inside signup.** The letter is the first
thing a member writes, so the first time they see the register it reads **1 / 57**, not 0.
Honest, and it is the highest-leverage single change in the funnel.

**Second consequence: chapter I now contains no multi-day trial.** SEVEN GLASSES moves to
Trial 10. Nine single-day trials, 24h apart, means a member clears nine things in their first
ten days — then meets the first week-long hold once the habit of returning already exists.
This resolves Rob's objection to a seven-day trial at position five.

## 22.2 The story — six acts

The member does not see this. It is what the trials are built to produce.

| Act | Trials | The turn | What they can do by the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I · FOUNDATION** | 01–09 | *You are not short of time. You are short of decisions.* | Walked 30 minutes without input. Finished something abandoned. Carried a load a mile. Written two sealed letters. |
| **II · DISCIPLINE** | 10–19 | *Showing up is a separate skill from trying hard.* | Held a daily action for a week and for ten days. Sat an hour with no input. Run a clock trial. Repeated Trial 06 and felt the difference. |
| **III · ENDURANCE** | 20–29 | *You have more capacity than you have been spending.* | An unbroken hour. Twenty-one consecutive days. Been among strangers doing the same thing. Asked three people what is wrong with them. |
| **IV · PRESSURE** | 30–38 | *Conditions are not a reason. They are the trial.* | Three hours on their feet. Trained twice in one day, before dawn, in weather they would have postponed for. |
| **V · MASTERY** | 39–47 | *Competence is owed outward.* | Navigated on paper. Learned first aid. Taught someone. Rested deliberately. Recorded an older relative's life. |
| **VI · ASCENSION** | 48–57 | *Do it where it counts, with someone who knows.* | Long solo ground with a witness and a check-in. Opened both letters. Finished. |

**The spine, in one line:** *decisions → showing up → capacity → conditions → competence →
consequence.*

Note what is deliberately absent: no act is about winning, beating anyone, or a number going
up. That is finding 4 applied.

## 22.4 What a member can honestly claim at the end

Rob's requirement was that people **see results**. These are the defensible ones — every
claim is something the trials literally required:

- **Physically:** walked or moved continuously for three hours; carried load for an hour; climbed 300m; cleared 100 reps in a day; trained twice in a day and before dawn.
- **Sharper:** an hour of no input, repeatedly; a day measuring nothing; a full day single-tasking; navigation without GPS.
- **More organised:** accounted for a day, a week of spending, ten kept promises, a rest day planned rather than collapsed into.
- **In themselves:** two sealed letters answered honestly six months later; criticism asked for and taken; one overdue conversation had; one refusal; a relative recorded; a day given to someone else.

**And 57 written accounts.** That is the book.
