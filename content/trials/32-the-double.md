**THE LINE**

Two easy sessions, four hours apart, in one day. Recovery between efforts is a skill of its
own, and this is the first time it's tested on purpose.

**THE WORK**

1. Do two separate movement sessions in one day, at least four hours apart — walking, the Stack, anything you've already done here.
2. Neither session needs to be hard. Talkable pace, same as always.
3. Fifteen to thirty minutes each is plenty — this trial is about the gap and the repetition, not the length of either session.
4. Eat and drink normally between the two. This isn't a fasting or restriction trial, it's about doing something twice in a day.
5. Log both sessions on the same day to clear it.

**THE RUBRIC**

*RISK* — Making the first session too hard and having nothing left for the second, or
skipping food between the two because the day got busy.

*GROUND* — Whatever either chosen movement needs — the same rules as whichever trials you're
drawing on.

*STOP* — The usual four constants apply to both sessions independently: chest pain or
tightness · breathlessness that doesn't settle within a minute of stopping · dizziness that
doesn't clear when you sit down · any sharp or new pain. A stop on session one doesn't
cancel session two later in the day, and vice versa.

*SCALE* — Two hours apart instead of four, if your day genuinely doesn't allow more
separation · ten minutes each instead of fifteen to thirty.

*CLEARED WHEN* — Two sessions are logged on the same day, at least the minimum gap apart.

**NOTE**

The second session almost always feels different from the first. That difference is the
point of the trial.
